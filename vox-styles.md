A style like this is a handful of independent settings, so you get a different look by changing two or three of them and keeping the rest. In the video I built, the settings are:

- **Palette:** off-white paper, black ink, one yellow accent, red for strikes.
- **Photo treatment:** black-and-white cutout with a white sticker edge on a colour block.
- **Type:** heavy sans headlines, italic serif for quotes, handwriting for annotations.
- **Texture:** paper grain plus film grain.
- **Motion:** stop-motion jitter on cutouts, stamped text, highlighter sweeps.
- **Transition:** one yellow sheet wiping across.
- **Devices:** globe with a route line, crossed-out paper, counter, stamp.

Five variants that still read as explainer-documentary:

1. **Archive / investigative:** dark background, manila folders, typewriter labels, red string and pins, photos as taped prints. Good for history and geopolitics.
2. **Map-led:** almost everything happens on maps, with muted land, one highlight colour, animated borders and arrows. Good for geopolitics and trade.
3. **Data-led:** clean white, thin grid, one accent, charts drawing themselves, big numbers. Good for finance and macro.
4. **Newspaper collage:** newsprint texture, torn headline strips, halftone photos, serif headlines, ink-blue accent. Good for book summaries.
5. **Notebook / blueprint:** grid paper, pencil and marker lines, sticky notes, hand-drawn arrows. Good for step-by-step lessons.

Ten more, all different from the five above. Each line gives the look, then what it suits.

1. **Evidence board:** corkboard, pinned photos, red thread linking them, handwritten tags. Suits conspiracies, networks of people, "who owns what".
2. **Redacted file:** white documents, black redaction bars lifting to reveal text, stamps, monospace type. Suits secrets, leaks, policy.
3. **Vintage textbook:** cream paper, engraved-style illustrations, numbered figures, thin serif captions. Suits science, history, mythology.
4. **Screen investigation:** browser windows, search bars, zooms into highlighted lines of an article or tweet. Suits news explainers and tech.
5. **Timeline ribbon:** one horizontal line running through the whole video, the camera sliding along it, events popping up as cards. Suits biographies and "how we got here".
6. **Split comparison:** frame cut in two, left versus right, mirrored layouts and two accent colours. Suits debates, before/after, country versus country.
7. **Money trail:** banknotes, receipts, coins and arrows showing flows, with a green and black palette. Suits finance, corruption, business models.
8. **Paper cut-out theatre:** layered coloured paper with depth and shadows, flat characters sliding in on sticks. Suits fables, religion stories, lessons for a young audience.
9. **Chalkboard:** dark green or black board, chalk lettering and diagrams drawn stroke by stroke, eraser smears as transitions. Suits concepts, economics, maths.
10. **Poster / propaganda:** bold two-colour print, big diagonal shapes, halftone portraits, slogans in condensed caps. Suits political history and strong opinions.

Two of these change the structure as well as the surface: the timeline ribbon and the split comparison decide how the whole script is laid out, so pick them before writing. The other eight can be applied to a script you already have.
